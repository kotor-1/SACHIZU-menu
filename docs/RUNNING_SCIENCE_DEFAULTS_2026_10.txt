# Running science defaults, October 2026

**Status:** implemented version of `rp-science-2026.10.0`, a separately versioned finite-policy authority. These are provisional engine defaults authorized for this phase; they are not retrospective coach approvals, are not entries in legacy `rs-2026.09.0`, and do not alter the prior `rp-2026.09.0` approval record. `ENGINE_POLICY` means the product selected a conservative operational value where the literature did not directly test that exact dose. Do not display such a value as a scientific optimum.

**Review date:** 2026-10-10. Primary/public source pages were checked directly. Haugen is a narrative review integrating science and elite best-practice; NSCA is a position statement; Moran is a meta-analysis. The confidence labels below are practical transfer/dose judgments, not formal GRADE ratings.

## Evidence anchors

Haugen et al. 2019 Table 2 lists elite sprint best-practice ranges: acceleration 10–50m, >98% Vmax, 2–7min rest, session 100–300m; maximal velocity 10–30m flying zone (run-in excluded), >98% Vmax, 4–15min, 50–150m fly volume; sprint-specific endurance 80–150m, >95% Vmax, 8–30min, 300–900m; speed endurance 60–80m, 90–95% Vmax, 2–4min between reps / 8–15min between sets, 600–2000m; tempo 100–300m, 60–70% Vmax, 1–3min, 1000–2000m. These are summary best-practice bands, not randomized dose-finding results. Haugen illustrates a young, relatively untrained acceleration session as 20m crouch-start reps with 2min rest; for maximal velocity, 20m buildup + 10m fly with about 4min; and for a relatively untrained junior, 2–3×100m sprint-specific endurance with 10min rest. The first two examples do not specify rep count. Haugen expressly says that the lowest effective speed-endurance intensity is not established and that evidence comparing periodization outcomes is lacking. Table percentages mean percent of maximal velocity, not subjective effort or percent of an event PB.

Moran et al. 2017 meta-analysis included healthy male organized-sport athletes, study-group mean ages 10–18, and interventions of 4–16 weeks. It found pooled improvement but varied responses by PHV group; the PRE-PHV confidence interval crossed zero. The abstract suggests twice-weekly sessions with up to 16 sprints around 20m and work:rest 1:25 or >90s. “Up to 16” is an upper recommendation, not an initial set count, elementary-specific trial, girls-specific result, or proof that PRE-PHV youth cannot improve.

NSCA LTAD (Lloyd et al. 2016) supports individualized and nonlinear youth development, motor-skill and foundational-strength development, monitoring, adequate recovery, and qualified professional/pedagogical oversight. It does not give an elementary sprint repetition prescription. It reports a cited suggestion of at least 48h rest before competition; use that as a conservative scheduling guard rather than evidence for a particular shakeout workout.

## Cross-cutting rules and limitations

- **ENGINE_POLICY — Composer retrieval safety boundary:** when age group is explicitly resolved as ELEMENTARY and `science.experience` is explicitly BEGINNER, AI-generated freeform menu candidates are limited to reviewed exercises with difficulty 1–2; missing and higher difficulty values are excluded. This conservative initial product filter is not an age-eligibility claim, does not narrow the full reviewed-ID set available for human selection, and does not restrict manual addition or editing.
- Keep `>95%` sprint-specific endurance and `90–95%` speed endurance as separate semantic methods. The 90–95% historical concept is not automatically a “black hole”: Haugen describes a coach-practice polarization belief but says its mechanisms and superiority are not established.
- A displayed percentage must name its denominator. `Vmax`, same-distance PB average speed, and perceived maximal intent are separate fields. Do not convert one into another without an explicit validated relationship.
- Proposed finite-policy recovery floor: at least 48h between high-intensity sprint sessions, at most two high-intensity sprint sessions per week, and a 48h precompetition high-intensity exclusion. These are conservative **ENGINE_POLICY** guards informed by practice/recommendations, not universal proven injury thresholds. The planner requires manual recent/next-session hours and count of high-intensity sessions over the last 7 days; if any are missing, it must not emit a high-intensity session. The current app may not account for all sport/competition load; a manually entered count is not a full load assessment.
- Explicit load reduction, when requested for an otherwise eligible prescription, halves planned repetitions rounded up (minimum one) while preserving distance and rest. This is an **ENGINE_POLICY** reduction rule, not a study-validated response curve.
- Stop/modify for pain, poor readiness, or technique/speed deterioration. Do not invent a universal percent drop-off threshold from these sources.
- For elementary children, require exact chronological age, stated experience, maturity (or explicit unknown), symptom-free readiness, and adult/coach supervision. Acceleration additionally requires sprint familiarity, age 10–12, experienced status and recent/next high-intensity load. Basic relaxed skills require neither a race event nor a sprint-load history. If a required gate is absent, no automatic prescription. Do not infer PHV or training age from grade.
- `rs-2026.09.0` legacy runtime ACTIVE status is immutable repository history, not an endorsement of these defaults. `rp-2026.09.0` confirmation records remain unchanged. These defaults belong only to `rp-science-2026.10.0`; do not import legacy RuleSet numeric envelopes or claim retroactive coach approval.
